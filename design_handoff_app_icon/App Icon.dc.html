Pelagic / app icon / pass 5
Porthole and sunken record, pushed further.
Riffs on 4c and 4d: two porthole variants, two record variants, and two that combine them.
5a
Porthole, half flooded
The glass is half under water. The equaliser stands on the waterline and its reflection breaks up in the ripples below.
5b
Porthole, record beyond
The two ideas combined: through the porthole, a record drifts in the light shafts.
5c
Porthole label
A full record where the centre label is a riveted porthole, with one point of light in the water behind it.
5d
Record, waterline
The record stands half in the water. Below the line it shifts and darkens with refraction, which keeps the original Aperture split.
5e
Record, seabed
The record lies on the seafloor in a pool of light, a trail of bubbles rising from it. More of a scene, with real depth.
5f
Record, caustics
A close-up of the record with rippling light from the surface playing across it. Shallower and calmer than the others.
Pelagic / app icon / pass 4
Six figurative icons, each one a real thing from the deep.
Still grayscale with white glow, but each one is a recognisable object with layered light and detail.
4a
Jellyfish
A glowing bell trailing tentacles that fade into the dark. Bioluminescence as the only light source, matching the glow language.
4b
Nautilus
A chambered shell drawn as one glowing spiral. It doubles as a cochlea, the spiral in the ear that hears.
4c
Porthole
A submarine porthole with an equaliser glowing on the other side of the glass. Looking out into your library.
4d
Sunken record
A vinyl record resting below the surface, lit by shafts of light, bubbles rising off its edge. The most literal "music at depth".
4e
Pearl
An open shell holding a single pearl. Your FLAC library as something kept and treasured on the seafloor.
4f
Moonlit waveform
A moon setting on the horizon over a sea drawn as audio waveforms, brightest where the moonlight falls.
Pelagic / app icon / pass 3
Six new marks, unrelated to Aperture.
Same material: near-black tile, white line work, one point of glow. Different ideas of depth.
3a
Lure
A single line drops from the surface and ends in a light, like an anglerfish lure. One gesture, very legible at 40px.
3b
Thermocline
Water layers that crowd together and darken with depth. Reads as a stack of tracks or a depth chart.
3c
Ascent
Three bubbles rising and swelling toward the surface. Friendlier than the others, still in the line-work style.
3d
Monogram
p
A lowercase p in General Sans Light with one glowing point beside it. Ties the icon directly to the type system.
3e
Photic zone
Light falling from the surface through drifting particles, with one bright point below. The app's atmosphere in a tile, no line work.
3f
Contour
Drifting concentric rings: a seafloor contour map and a record's grooves at once. Echoes the contour layer behind every screen.
Pelagic / app icon / pass 2
Six takes on the Aperture icon.
Each keeps the ring, the horizon and the monochrome glow, and gives the half below the line its own presence. Shown at 200px, then at 60 and 40px on a mid-tone wallpaper to check the lower half still reads.
Current
Lower arc at 20%
2a
Refraction
Below the line the ring bends sideways, the way an oar looks broken in water. The lower arc goes up to 60% and the shift makes it read as intentional.
2b
Submerged
The lower half fills with a faint wash of light that fades with depth. It turns the half-ring into a solid shape, so it holds up at small sizes.
2c
Sonar
The lower arc repeats outward and fades, like a sonar ping or sound moving through water. It is the most musical of the six.
2d
Marine snow
The lower arc breaks into particles, picking up the marine snow drifting behind every screen. Stroke above, dots below.
2e
Last light
The ring becomes a solid disc half under the surface, with its reflection breaking up below. The boldest option and the easiest to spot on a home screen.
2f
Bioluminescent
Flips the hierarchy: the upper arc dims and the lower arc glows, as if the light comes from the deep. The ring is one continuous stroke.
